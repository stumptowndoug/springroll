Tasks — what a row should tell you

six directions · real tasks, real runs from the dev database

The list as built shows configuration and telemetry: name, description, schedule, three verbs, seven bars. It never shows what a task produced, and never shows what it is made of — model, integrations, outputs. These six studies each attack one of those gaps. Rows are the seven live tasks in the workspace; times, costs, models and report text are pulled from the actual runs (two excerpts for tasks with no stored report are marked †).

0 · As built — the baseline, for contrast

What is on the screen today. Every row costs four lines and tells you nothing you didn't already know when you wrote the task.

B · Anatomy grid — a fixed grammar of parts, scannable down the column

Why this pair: A supplies the content the page has never had, B supplies the composition your long-term list needs a home for, and D keeps the depth one click away instead of on screen. All three live inside max-w-4xl and inside the two-size type discipline.

Open question: whether the anatomy line survives contact with a workspace of forty tasks, or whether it should appear on hover only. Worth testing at that scale before committing.

What to decide: whether the row's job is to report output (A), composition (B), or schedule (C) — the other two follow from that choice. My pick is F, with C as a second view once there are enough scheduled tasks to make the day look populated.
