Calendar ⇄ list — switching, groups, and the indicator

status keeps the color · group gets structure

With status keeping green/red/amber, group can't be a hue — two colour systems on one surface and you read neither. That's fine, because colour is the weakest of the four channels available anyway.

Four ways to express group without spending colour:
  1. Position — group becomes a lane in the calendar or a section in the list. Structure, not decoration. Strongest, and free.
  2. Filter — group is how you narrow the view rather than something drawn on it. Costs nothing at rest.
  3. A faint label — research › in faint, at the far edge of the row. Reads only when you look for it.
  4. Shape — circle / square / diamond per group. Works for three, fails at six, and nobody learns the legend.
Your data argues for the first two: 7 recipes across just two folders — research (3), analytics (1) — with 3 unfiled. A colour system for two groups is over-engineering; lanes and a filter cost nothing and get better as you add folders. Note group is a path up to four levels (Marketing/Content), so whatever you pick has to survive nesting — another reason hue is the wrong channel.

The switcher — three treatments, same three views

Small decision, high frequency. It sits at the right of the page head opposite the title, and it should read as a lens on one dataset rather than as navigation between pages.

Segmented ListWeekMonth Most obvious, most furniture. The border box is heavier than anything else on a page built from hairlines.
Dotted text List · Week · Month Matches the meta-line grammar already used everywhere else. Quiet enough to sit next to a 28px title without competing.
Icons Compact and works on mobile, but list-vs-week-vs-month icons are close to indistinguishable at 15px without labels.

Pick: dotted text. It's the only one that doesn't introduce a new component to a page whose whole discipline is "no boxes", and the active item can carry the same ink-vs-muted weight contrast the rest of the app uses.

Week A · Time grid — group as a filter, not a mark

The literal calendar. Rows are fire times, columns are days, colour is status only. Group lives entirely in the chip row above: click research and everything else drops out. Nothing on the grid encodes folder, so the grid stays legible when you have eleven folders.

Month · the pattern view — one dot per report, colour is status only

Zoom out and individual reports stop mattering; what you're reading is the texture. Six dots a day, red where something broke, hollow where it hasn't happened yet. Group stays in the chip filter — narrowing to analytics leaves one dot per day and turns this into a health calendar for that folder.

The indicator — six replacements for the seven bars

Same row, same recipe, six different right-hand marks. The current bars encode cost per day by height, which nobody reads as cost — so whatever replaces them should encode one thing and say so plainly.

1 · Five dotsthe approved spec
Daily Google Trends Report
Calm and already specified. Five days is a short memory, and equal dots make a three-week streak look identical to a good week.
2 · Uptime ribbon14 days, one segment each
Daily Google Trends Report
Instantly legible, borrowed from every status page ever. Reads slightly "ops dashboard", which may be the wrong register for a reading product.
3 · Micro-month4 weeks, mirrors the Month view
Daily Google Trends Report
The one that ties the views together — the row indicator is literally a thumbnail of the Month calendar, so switching feels like zooming rather than teleporting.
4 · Duration sparklinewith failure ticks
Daily Google Trends Report
Shows drift — a recipe getting slower or pricier over weeks. Beautiful, but it answers a question you rarely ask, and a single failure is easy to miss.
5 · State chipa word, not a picture
Daily Google Trends Report healthy · 28 runs
The only accessible-by-default option and the only one that survives being read aloud. Says nothing about history, so it needs pairing.
6 · Next upforward, not backward
Daily Google Trends Report next in 17h
The most useful single fact on a scheduling page, and the one thing none of the other five tell you. Almost too minimal on its own.

Pick: 3 + 5 together — the micro-month carries history and visually rhymes with the calendar view, and the state chip carries the same fact in words for accessibility and for the failing case ("failing 3 days"). Add 6's "next in 17h" to the meta line where the schedule string already lives, and the row answers past, present and future without a chart.

List view — sections by folder, micro-month on the right

The non-calendar half of the switch. Group is expressed by section — channel 1, and the app already has a Flat/Grouped preference to hang it on. Status keeps all the colour. The indicator is the calendar in miniature, so the two views feel like one idea.

Card view — same grammar, if you want a third lens

If cards stay in the mix, they should use the identical parts — folder as the section heading, state chip, micro-month. Not a different design language, just a different density.
