The masthead, set four ways.

The structure is decided: five words where the running head lives, current page in ink. What's left is typography — how the nav separates itself from the content without raising its voice. Same layout in all four; only the type changes. The Mobile nav switch compares keeping the words on phones against a hamburger menu. Note: the serif and mono here approximate the app's Source Serif 4 and JetBrains Mono with system faces.

Notes · Quiet sans

Zero new voices in the app. The risk is under-separation: at 12.5px regular, faint words could be mistaken for a caption until hovered. Safest choice; least memorable.

Notes · Small caps

Letterspacing + uppercase is the strongest "this is chrome, not content" signal typography can send without color or surfaces. Tiny size keeps it whisper-quiet; tap targets rely on padding, which the prototype includes.

Notes · Machine

Borrowing the schedule mono means the masthead needs no new font and inherits an existing meaning: "system information." Fits Spring Roll's task-runner soul; slightly cool in temperature.

Notes · Literary

The only treatment with real warmth, and the middots make five words read as one composed line. Caveat: when a user picks the reader (serif) font preference, nav and content share a face and the separation softens.
