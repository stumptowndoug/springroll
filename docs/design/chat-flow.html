Design spec · The pick, assembled

A thin bar to speak, a full page to talk, one inbox to remember.

This is the recommendation, no longer a variation: C's record model with a thin launcher bar. The bar sits at the bottom of every screen and does exactly one thing — take a sentence. Press ⏎ and you're on a full-screen thread page, tagged with the screen you asked from. That thread is instantly a record in Inbox under Asked — which is the whole chat history, no Chat tab, no archive. Run letters don't need the bar at all: their reply composer is already the thread.

The bar never grows a panel. That's the discipline that keeps it thin. It's a launcher, like a command line: the conversation itself always happens at full width, where letters, credential cards, and tool activity have room. One gesture in, one honest surface.

The tag is the contract. In step 3 the thread wears “◦ Morning digest” in its running head — that chip is simultaneously the scope (what the thread can see and change), the link back to where you were, and the label its Inbox row carries forever (“Asked · Morning digest”). A thread started from Inbox just has no chip: general scope, tagged to nothing.

BarOne bar, dressed by where it stands

The bar is identical everywhere — 46px, pill, one line. Only two things change: the chip (the page's subject, if it has one) and the placeholder, which names what asking can do here. That placeholder is where “chat does everything” lives — nobody reads a manifesto, everybody reads a placeholder.

Where the bar is absent

Thread pages and run letters — their reply composer is the bar, full width. Two speaking surfaces on one screen would be the old “all over the place” again. Settings keeps the bar (general scope); nothing else is exempt.

The chip is removable

Backspace on an empty bar (or click the chip's ×, not mocked) drops the scope — same bar, now a general ask. You never navigate somewhere else to ask a different kind of question.

RulesThe grammar, and what it deletes

Six rules

1. The bar takes a sentence; ⏎ always opens a full-screen thread. 2. Every thread is tagged with its origin — chip in the head, “Asked · subject” in its row. 3. Every thread is an Inbox record the moment it starts. 4. “Asked” in Inbox is the chat history — all of it. 5. Letters and threads are one surface; letters just take their first reply later. 6. Facts on pages say Ask, and asking is always the primary path; forms are the fallback.

Deleted by this design

The Chat tab and /chat as home · the chat index page · every scattered ChatContextButton · “Ask about this run” (replaced by replying to the letter) · the separate integration composer (the bar on Connections, scoped) · the recipe composer as a distinct surface (the bar on Recipes: “describe a new recipe”).

Ship order

1 — reply composer on run letters (smallest change, loudest signal). 2 — the bar + full-screen thread page with tags; kill the Chat tab, route / to Inbox. 3 — Asked filter; migrate old chat sessions into the feed. 4 — Ask affordances on facts, page by page. Each step stands alone.

Open questions for dogfood

Does ⏎-to-full-screen feel heavy for tiny asks (“pause this”)? If so the answer is a faster acknowledgment — the thread opens already answered — never a mini panel. And does Inbox need the thread rows collapsed after they resolve? (The receipt-row idea from the IA study is compatible if it silts up.)
